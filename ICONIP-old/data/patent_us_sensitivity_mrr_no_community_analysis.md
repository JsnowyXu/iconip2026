# Patent-US Non-Community Sensitivity Analysis

The non-community sensitivity results on Patent-US show that the model is more sensitive to sampling and neighborhood settings in the inductive setting than in the transductive setting. Across the four examined parameters, inductive MRR ranges from 0.8998 to 0.9059, while transductive MRR remains within a narrower interval from 0.9471 to 0.9482. This gap is consistent with the different evaluation regimes: inductive prediction relies more heavily on robust local evidence and training-time negative construction, whereas transductive prediction benefits from a more stable node set and therefore exhibits smaller fluctuations.

Among the negative-sampling parameters, `popular_negative_ratio` has the largest effect on the inductive task. Increasing it to 0.1 yields the best inductive MRR of 0.9059, an improvement of about 0.0055 over the anchor MRR. This suggests that introducing a moderate proportion of popularity-aware negatives provides a useful hard-negative signal: the model is encouraged to distinguish true endpoints from plausible high-frequency alternatives, rather than only separating positives from uniformly sampled nodes. However, the trend is not monotonic, as 0.05 and 0.2 are weaker than 0.1, indicating that too few popular negatives may be insufficient while too many can distort the negative distribution.

The effect of `typed_negative_ratio` is comparatively mild in the inductive task but clearer in the transductive task. The best transductive result is obtained at 0.2, with an MRR of 0.9482. This indicates that type-consistent negative samples remain informative even when the test nodes are largely known: by matching the node type of the true destination, these negatives reduce shortcuts based on type information and force the model to rely more on temporal and relational compatibility.

For recent-neighbor context, the optimal window differs between the two task types. The inductive task prefers a larger window of 32 recent interactions, while the transductive task performs best at 16. This suggests that unseen or sparsely observed inductive cases benefit from broader local history, whereas transductive prediction can rely on a more compact context. Very large or poorly matched windows may introduce stale or weakly relevant events, which explains why the best setting is not simply the maximum in both cases.

The aggregation strategy is the least sensitive parameter in these results. Attention aggregation is slightly better than mean aggregation for both task types, but the difference is small, especially in the transductive setting. This implies that the recent-neighbor representations are already relatively homogeneous for Patent-US, so the choice between attention and averaging changes performance only marginally. Still, attention remains a reasonable default because it gives the model the capacity to emphasize more relevant historical interactions without harming performance here.

Overall, the Patent-US sensitivity study supports a moderate hard-negative strategy and task-dependent neighbor-window selection. For the inductive setting, `popular_negative_ratio=0.1`, `typed_negative_ratio=0.1`, `recent_neighbor_size=32`, and attention aggregation provide the strongest non-community configuration. For the transductive setting, the best settings are `popular_negative_ratio=0.2`, `typed_negative_ratio=0.2`, `recent_neighbor_size=16`, and attention aggregation. The larger swings observed in the inductive task indicate that hyperparameter choices should be tuned separately for inductive and transductive evaluation rather than transferred directly between them.
